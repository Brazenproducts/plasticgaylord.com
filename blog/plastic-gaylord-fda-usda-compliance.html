FDA and USDA Compliance for Plastic Gaylords: A Buyer's Checklist

Plasticgaylord Editorial • October 2026

Compliance Is a Sourcing Decision, Not an Afterthought

For food and pharmaceutical operations, the packaging that touches product is part of the regulated supply chain. Plastic gaylords used to hold meat, poultry, produce, or ingredients must meet FDA requirements for food-contact materials, and facilities under USDA inspection must be able to document that their containers are sanitary, non-contaminating, and cleanable. Getting compliance right at the sourcing stage avoids the far more expensive problem of a failed audit or a product recall traced to packaging.

This guide gives procurement teams a practical checklist for specifying compliant plastic gaylords, so the units you buy hold up to inspection as well as they hold up on the dock.

Start With the Resin

Compliance begins at the material. Food-contact gaylords must be molded from FDA-compliant polypropylene, meaning the base resin and any additives or colorants conform to the relevant food-contact substance regulations. Recycled or uncertified resin is a common shortcut that creates compliance gaps; for direct food contact, insist on virgin or certified food-grade PP with documentation.

Design Features That Pass Inspection

USDA and food-safety auditors evaluate whether a container can actually be cleaned and kept sanitary in practice. The design of the gaylord matters as much as the material:

  • Smooth, non-porous interior surfaces that do not trap residue.
  • Rounded internal corners and seams that drain and dry fully.
  • No absorbent components that can harbor bacteria or moisture.
  • Chemical resistance to the sanitizers and wash-down chemistry in use.
  • Clear, moldable surfaces that resist staining and odor retention.

These features are exactly where plastic outperforms fiber. A fiber gaylord cannot be sanitized and reused in a food plant; a PP gaylord can be washed, inspected, and returned to service hundreds of times. Facilities running mixed packaging often pair gaylords with PP corrugated bulk boxes so every food-contact container in the building meets the same hygiene standard.

Documentation Procurement Should Require

An audit is won on paperwork as much as on practice. When you source plastic gaylords, build a document packet that includes the resin food-contact compliance statement, the manufacturer's declaration of conformity, and recommended cleaning and sanitation guidance. If your product is exported or imported, confirm country-of-origin and duty status as well. Custom Builds and Compliance

Standard gaylords rarely match a specific food line perfectly, and void space or overstacking can itself become a contamination and damage risk. Custom-sized PP gaylords let you match your pallet footprint, fill level, and sanitation equipment exactly. Because Elipacko builds to custom dimensions with FDA-compliant resin, you can specify a compliant container that also optimizes cube and handling rather than compromising one for the other.

Putting the Checklist to Work

For a compliant plastic gaylord program, procurement should confirm five things before issuing a PO: food-grade resin with documentation, a cleanable and inspection-ready design, available declarations of conformity, clear duty and origin status, and the option to custom-size for the application. Source from a direct manufacturer that can deliver all five, and compliance stops being a scramble before each audit and becomes a built-in feature of your packaging standard.
